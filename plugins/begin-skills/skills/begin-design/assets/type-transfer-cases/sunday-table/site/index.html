A cake in the middle of the table is an invitation with very few instructions. Bring a plate. Move the cups a little. Cut a second slice if the conversation runs long.

This notebook begins with a lemon: the peel we notice first, the juice we measure, and the scent left on our hands. Beside it is a small exercise in sharing a recipe—how much to set out when four becomes eight.

Notes from the citrus drawer

Three small observations to take into the kitchen

01 · The whole lemon

Before the first cut

A lemon has two very different kinds of usefulness. The peel carries its perfume; the juice brings a sharper voice. They deserve separate lines in a notebook, even when they begin as the same piece of fruit.

For this worksheet, the lemon juice is measured in millilitres. The number of lemons is left open: fruit varies, so a shopping count would imply a precision this ingredient list does not have.

02 · On measuring

Let the units do their work

Flour and sugar stay in grams. Oil, milk and juice stay in millilitres. A gram and a millilitre answer different questions; this worksheet scales each quantity in its own unit.

03 · A margin worth keeping

Leave yourself a note

“Set out the jug before starting.” “Bring the green plate.” A recipe note can remember the human part of a table as well as the numbers.

A place for the ordinary meal

Sunday Table is a fictional journal of kitchen observations, useful measurements and things to remember. Its pages move between the pleasure of reading and the small practical work of getting ready.

The Lemon & olive oil cake ingredient list is an illustrative calculation exercise. It has not been kitchen-tested and includes no baking method. All journal writing and artwork are original example material.
