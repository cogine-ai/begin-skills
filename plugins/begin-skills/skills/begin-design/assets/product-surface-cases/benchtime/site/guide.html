Your first request

  1. Choose your day and machine. The planner covers Saturday 7 and Sunday 8 November 2026. Every time shown is local to Dublin.
  2. Find a free half-hour. Green tiles are available; striped tiles are occupied. Click a time to start, then choose an end time in the session editor. On a phone, the editor comes into view; use “Back to the time grid” to reconsider.
  3. Say what you’re making. Include enough context for staff to understand the project. Allow time for setup and tidying inside your session.
  4. Add it to your request. Add up to three sessions, then check the dates, times, purposes and total. Save a draft or export a file to keep.

Try the example: Saturday 7 November, Laser cutter, 10:00–11:00, “Market stall sign for Róisín O’Connell”. One hour is estimated at €18.00.

Keep the plan workable

Striped occupied time cannot be added. Your own sessions cannot overlap on the same day, even on different machines. Back-to-back sessions are allowed: ending at 11:00 and starting at 11:00 is fine.

Use Edit beside a session to change its day, machine, times or purpose, then choose Update session. The original stays in your request until the edit passes the checks. Cancel edit leaves that original untouched. Remove takes just that session out of the working request.

Orange tiles show the time you are selecting. Dark green tiles are already in your request; an orange outline and dot mark your current selection even when it overlaps that original. Changing a day or machine changes the availability view, and keeps the request below.

Keep a copy of your work

Save draft makes a checkpoint in this browser’s local storage. Restore saved draft replaces the working request and current selection with that checkpoint. Save again when you want the checkpoint to include your latest changes. The planner shows the saved time in Dublin, its session count, and whether current work differs before you restore.

Your working changes are also kept for this browser tab, so visiting the guide and coming back does not normally lose them. Local storage can be blocked or cleared; a failure is shown beside the draft controls. Keep an exported file if the plan matters.

Export request downloads a readable HTML file you can open in a browser. Export CSV downloads the same dates, machines, purposes, times and estimates for a spreadsheet. Both include Dublin time and the complete request total.

This is a fictional, local scheduling demonstration. A saved draft or exported request is not submitted, approved or booked. No staff member receives it, and no workshop time is reserved.
