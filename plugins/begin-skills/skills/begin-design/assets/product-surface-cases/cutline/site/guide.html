The CUTLINE guide / About 4 minutes

A careful pass,
from cue to file

One scene. Three cues. A clear decision about the version you keep. Here’s how to make your way through the editing room.

01 / Choose a clip

Start with a whole scene

The clip selector opens The last ferry or Glasshouse morning. Each contains three complete sample cues. A cue is one subtitle entry: its text, start time and end time.

The public example opens The last ferry in the editor, with the same line and timing. If you’ve worked on that clip before, your saved local draft appears. Switching clips preserves each clip’s own selected cue, edits and review decisions.

THE LAST FERRY / CUE 02

Maëlle waits by the water.

00:00:03,200→00:00:06,000

The frames are original illustrations. Selecting a cue changes its words and time in the frame study; the timeline selects cues, with gaps showing the pauses. There is no video playback, audio or automatic transcription.

02 / Read and adjust

Give every word its time

Select a numbered cue in the timeline or cue sheet. The editing panel shows the whole subtitle, and the frame study shows that same text. Previous and next arrows offer another route through the three cues.

Type directly into Subtitle text, Start time or End time. Your changes take effect as you type. Text and timing edits start a new revision and clear all review marks and approval for that clip.

  • Use HH:MM:SS,mmm: hours, minutes, seconds, then three milliseconds. For example, 00:00:03,200 means 3.2 seconds.
  • Minutes and seconds must be between 00 and 59. The end must be later than the start.
  • A cue must begin at or after the previous cue ends. Adjacent cues may touch, but cannot overlap.
  • Each cue needs text. Line breaks are kept in the SRT; empty lines inside one cue are blocked because SRT readers use blank lines as entry separators.
If an error appears, the draft keeps your input so you can correct it. Cue review, approval and export are blocked until all errors in the current clip are fixed.

On a phone, use Edit cue, Cue sheet and Review & export at the top of the room to move between the work and its final decision.

03 / Make the call

A suggestion needs your judgment

The first cue in The last ferry has one clearly labeled sample suggestion: change “Its” to “It’s.” This is a local example rule for the supplied line’s “It is” meaning. It is not an AI transcript or a claim that every use of “Its” is wrong.

Accept suggestion changes the actual cue to “It’s the last boat home.” Reject leaves the original words and records your decision. Both start a revision that you can review.

Undo decision restores the original wording and the pending choice. If you edit the line yourself, your wording becomes the working text and the sample rule is set aside. You can change that text directly, just like any other cue.

04 / Review a revision

Approve the words you actually checked

Once a cue reads correctly and its timing is valid, choose Mark cue reviewed. Resolve the sample suggestion before reviewing cue 01 of The last ferry. Use the cue sheet to select and read the other cues, then mark each one.

The review panel names the current clip and revision, and shows which of its three cues are checked. Each cue button takes you straight to that cue’s editing card and places keyboard focus on its heading. After checking it, use Back to review & export below the review button to return to the revision panel. When all three are reviewed and valid, Approve revision becomes available. That approval applies to the exact words, timing and suggestion decision you just reviewed.

Any later text or timing change clears every review mark and the old approval for that clip. Removing a review mark also clears approval. A new revision needs a complete pass before export. Approval of The last ferry never approves Glasshouse morning.

05 / Take the file

Three complete cues, one SRT

After approval, choose Download approved SRT. CUTLINE creates a real UTF-8 file with all three current cues: a sequence number, full start and end times, and the complete subtitle text. Accents in Maëlle and Noé, and the curly apostrophe in “It’s,” are kept.

EXAMPLE SRT ENTRY / AFTER ACCEPTING THE SUGGESTION

1
00:00:00,000 --> 00:00:03,000
It’s the last boat home.

The file name includes the clip and revision, such as last-ferry-r02.srt. Check your browser’s downloads for the file. You can open it in a text editor or import it into a video editor that supports SRT. A local download does not upload, publish or deliver subtitles to anyone.

06 / Keep your place

Leave a way back

CUTLINE automatically saves both clips, their selected cues and review state in this browser. The draft status confirms when that write succeeds. Going to the guide and back, or reloading the editor, restores the saved work.

Save checkpoint keeps a deliberate snapshot of both clips. You can keep editing, then choose Restore checkpoint to replace the current work with that saved snapshot. A confirmation tells you that later changes will be replaced. The snapshot includes the exact review state that belonged to those words.

Reset this clip restores only the current clip’s original three cues. It clears that clip’s edits, suggestion decisions, review marks and approval. The other clip and the saved checkpoint stay available. Reset asks for confirmation.

Browser drafts are not a backup service. Clearing browser data, changing browsers or devices, or using private browsing can remove them. If storage is blocked or full, the editor keeps your current work in the tab and warns before navigation. Keep the tab open and finish a valid export.
Scope & credits

A small tool, honestly framed

CUTLINE is an original fictional product study for independent documentary makers. This edition works on two local sample clips. It has no media import, real video playback, AI service, account, cloud sync, collaboration or publication connection.

All frame illustrations were drawn for this study. The sample wording and accented names are supplied fictional material. Nothing is presented as a customer quote, a completed external review or a published film.
