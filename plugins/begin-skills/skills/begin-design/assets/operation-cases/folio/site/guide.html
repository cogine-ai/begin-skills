The study room / A short guide

A guide to looking

One object.
As many ways of seeing it
as there are people.

01 / Begin with a detail

The hush vessel is a fictional ceramic artwork by the imagined maker Maëlle Dorian. The object, illustration, and exhibition were created for this little study room. Its folds, blue interior, and pale foot are starting points for your own observations.

Select a numbered pin on the illustration or choose Inspect this detail in the field notes. Three sample notes are already saved. You can change them, remove them, or leave them as a beginning.

There is no correct interpretation. Try describing an edge, following a shadow, or noticing where your eye returns.

02 / Place a pin

  1. Choose Add a pin. A new draft appears at the center. This study can hold up to twelve pins.
  2. Tap a place on the illustration to move it there. You can also drag a pin directly.
  3. For an ordinary button route, select the pin and choose Move anchor, then tap the illustration. Choose Finish placement to leave it where it is.
  4. For keyboard control, focus a numbered pin and use the ← ↑ ↓ → keys. Each press moves 1%; hold Shift to move 5%. Or enter an exact percentage in Across and Down; the small drawing beside these fields shows the selected anchor as it changes. The Full drawing link returns to the main illustration.

Anchors stay between 4% and 96% of the illustration so their markers remain visible. These are positions on a drawing, rather than measurements of a real object. In placement mode, Enter on the illustration centers the pin and moves focus to its name. Escape ends placement.

03 / Make a checkpoint

Give your detail a name of up to 64 characters and an observation of up to 600 characters. Save note keeps its current name, words, and position as a checkpoint. A green Saved label confirms it.

Further changes carry a Draft label and a dashed pin edge. Cancel changes returns that note to its saved checkpoint. For a new pin, Discard new pin removes the unfinished draft. Switching to another note preserves the changes you were making.

Remove pin removes its saved and draft versions together. Use Undo removal above the field notes to restore the most recently removed pin. A second removal replaces that single undo step.

04 / Come back to your work

The study automatically keeps a local working copy, including drafts, selection, and the last removal. Following this guide and returning, or reloading the study, recovers that copy when browser storage is available.

It belongs to this browser and site address. It is not synced to another device or uploaded. Private browsing, clearing site data, or a different address may leave you without the copy.

If local storage is blocked or full, the desk says that recovery is unavailable. A failed save keeps your draft in the open page and shows an error. Keep that page open and export your study before leaving. The browser may warn you before navigation while the work has no local recovery.

05 / Keep an annotated study

Export annotated study prepares one self-contained HTML file. It contains the original illustration, all current pin positions, and every complete observation. Each note is marked as a saved checkpoint or a draft. A blank draft is included with an explicit “No observation entered” label.

Open the downloaded file in a browser, then select an anchor to reach its note. The numbered links retain their reading size on a narrow screen, and the note index provides another route to each complete observation. A Print button provides an ordinary paper route. The exported file uses built-in browser fonts, works without a network connection, and can be shared by you.

The export is a readable record, rather than a workspace backup. It cannot be imported for further editing. Continue editing in the original browser. Your notes may include personal information, so read the file before choosing to share it.
