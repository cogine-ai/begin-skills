01 Make the first note

Open A seat for the unhurried city at 01 · A pause. Its highlighted sentence and note field sit together in The permission to pause.

Try this exact passage →

The selected sentence is exactly:

A pause becomes public when the place allows us to stay without explaining why.

Write the following in Your note on Passage 1, beneath that sentence:

Sample note text

This is the difference between a place to pass through and a place to belong. Keep the permission to linger in the design.

Choose Save note. The note field accepts 1–600 characters after surrounding spaces are removed. A blank note shows an error beside the field and keeps your input.

Expected: the note appears directly beneath the lavender sentence, marked Your note · Passage 1 and Saved in this browser. In an otherwise empty sample, the toolbar shows 1 saved note. The next step is to read on, or use Edit to change your thought.

If you already have a thought on this passage, you can keep it and continue with the editing steps. The counts below assume this is your only saved note. Step 5 explains the optional reset for trying this sample again.

02 Return to the sentence

Reload the essay in the same browser at the same site address. Your saved note remains attached to Passage 1. Reload does not create another note. Choose 02 · A welcome and then 01 · A pause to see that the thought stays with its sentence.

The three selectors correspond to these exact sentences:

  1. A pause: “A pause becomes public when the place allows us to stay without explaining why.”
  2. A welcome: “A good shared place leaves room for a person who has not yet been imagined.”
  3. An empty seat: “The empty seat is part of the offer: it leaves the next arrival a place in the story.”

Expected: the selected sentence is highlighted, its passage button is pressed, and its note controls sit directly below it. Choosing a different passage does not move or erase a saved note.

03 Change your thought

On Passage 1, choose Edit. Replace the note with this exact text:

Edited note text

Keep the permission to linger. A bench can welcome someone without asking them to become a customer.

Before saving, you can choose another passage and return; the current page keeps your draft. Cancel edit restores the saved wording. Reload also restores the saved wording and discards unsaved edits.

Choose Save changes to replace the saved note. You can keep one note on each of the three passages.

Expected: the edited text replaces the original text beneath Passage 1. The toolbar still shows 1 saved note. Reload keeps the edited text. Undo last change restores the original sample note.

04 Remove a note, or recover it

Choose Delete beside your saved note. The sentence stays in the essay and the note field becomes empty. A status message reports which passage changed.

Choose Undo last change beside the active note to restore it. Proofnote keeps one recoverable operation: your most recent save, edit, deletion, or reset. A new operation replaces that recovery point. Changing passage alone does not replace it.

Expected: deletion changes the count to 0 saved notes. Undo restores the exact note and returns the count to 1 saved note. With normal local storage, the recovery point also survives reload.

05 Know where the note lives

If you have already tried the sample and want to try the walkthrough again from an empty state, Reset notes is optional. You can also continue with your existing thoughts. Reset clears all three Proofnote notes and returns to Passage 1. Its recovery point contains the notes from before the reset; Undo last change restores them. Reset affects only this Proofnote sample.

Notes are saved in this browser for the current site address. There is no account, server copy, upload, or cloud sync. Moving to another browser or changing the site address starts a separate local sample. Clearing site data, or leaving a private browsing session, can remove the only saved copy.

If browser storage is blocked or full, the essay shows a warning and labels notes Kept on this page. You can still try the flow, but reload loses those changes. If stored sample data cannot be read, it stays untouched until you choose Reset notes; reset then starts a clean sample. Use fictional text here.

Expected: ordinary saving says Saved in this browser. A storage failure gives a specific warning and does not claim that a note will survive reload.
