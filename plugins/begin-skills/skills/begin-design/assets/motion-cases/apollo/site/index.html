01 / DEPARTURE

Three people, one outward journey

16 July 19699:32 a.m. EDT · Launch Pad 39A

On 16 July 1969, a Saturn V launched Apollo 11 from Launch Pad 39A at Kennedy Space Center. The launch clock read 9:32 a.m. EDT. Neil Armstrong, Buzz Aldrin and Michael Collins were aboard. [1]

Their journey would divide into two closely related tasks. Armstrong and Aldrin would use Eagle to reach the Moon’s surface. Collins would remain with Columbia in lunar orbit. The three crew members belong in the story together, even when the diagrams show their paths apart. [2]

02 / LUNAR ORBIT

The Moon becomes the centre

19 July 1969Lunar orbit insertion

Apollo 11 entered lunar orbit on 19 July 1969. The journey had reached a new frame of reference: the Moon. [1]

Keep the two vehicle names in view. Columbia would remain with Collins in orbit, while Eagle would carry Armstrong and Aldrin toward the surface. [2]

03 / LANDING

Two descend, one stays in orbit

20 July 1969Sea of Tranquility

Eagle landed on 20 July 1969 in the Sea of Tranquility. [1] Armstrong and Aldrin were with Eagle; Collins remained with Columbia in lunar orbit. [2]

The drawing separates the two vehicles so the relationship stays visible: a landing on the surface and a companion in orbit.

04 / RETURN

The journey closes on Earth

24 July 196912:50 p.m. EDT · Pacific splashdown

The crew returned to Earth with a Pacific splashdown on 24 July 1969 at 12:50 p.m. EDT. USS Hornet recovered them. [1]

The last chapter brings the three crew names together again: Neil Armstrong, Buzz Aldrin and Michael Collins. A landing is one moment; this is the shape of the whole journey.
