01

Read the paired scene

On the Read page, begin at P01 and continue to P04. English is the original language; French is the translation. The shared paragraph ID identifies one pair even when the two paragraphs have different lengths.

On a wide screen, the paired passages sit beside each other. On a phone, read the English and then the French under the same ID before moving to the next pair. The paragraph shortcuts take you to a particular part of the scene.

You can first read only the English, then return to compare. The editorial note follows the complete scene; it does not give the story away before you begin.

02

Follow one phrase

Try P03. The English says touched my sleeve, very lightly; the French says a effleuré ma manche. Read the sentence before and after the phrase in each language.

A reading question

Does the verb effleurer give you the same sense of restraint as the English phrase? Would adding an adverb make the gesture more explicit than you want?

Listen for who acts, what remains unstated, and how the next sentence carries the scene forward. A phrase can be close in meaning while changing the rhythm. The three phrase notes are invitations to make an editorial choice.

03

Edit and prepare a proof

  1. Open the Proof desk. It starts with the complete original scene and translation.
  2. Change the Proof title or one of the English or French paragraphs. Keep all four pairs. The writer and translator credits remain with the work.
  3. Use Editorial note to record your reading. You can leave it empty. For example, add: P03: I kept “a effleuré” because the gesture should stay slight.
  4. Select Prepare proof. The complete proof appears immediately after the preparation area.

Look for your title, P01–P04 in order, every full English and French paragraph, and your note. Nothing in a passage is shortened.

Preparation arranges your text; it does not translate it or judge its quality. Read the prepared passages again before keeping the proof.

Select Inspect the exact download text to see the entire plain-text document. Download text makes a UTF-8 file with the same text, including accents, punctuation, spaces and line breaks.

If you edit after preparation, the earlier proof stays visible with Earlier proof · edits pending. Prepare again to make the result current. Returning an edit to its previous words still requires preparation.

04

Keep and recover your reading

When the proof is current, select Keep this proof. This deliberately stores one complete proof and its editable text in this browser. Keeping again replaces that one copy.

Reset to original restores the original title, all eight passages and the editorial note. It clears the proof currently shown, but the kept copy remains available.

Select Recover kept proof to replace the current editable text and output with the complete copy you kept. Your title, note, paragraph text and exact prepared document return together.

Try recovery

Prepare a proof with your own note and keep it. Reload the Proof desk page. The original scene appears first and a recovery message offers the kept copy. Choose Recover kept proof; your edited note and its prepared proof return. Recovery does not happen automatically.

Forget kept copy removes only COMMON TONGUE’s stored proof. It leaves the text and result currently on the page intact. Other sites’ stored work is unaffected.

05

Correct a blocked proof

A proof needs a nonempty title and a nonempty English and French paragraph in every pair. The IDs must occur exactly once in the order P01, P02, P03, P04. The IDs stay attached to the paragraph fields; they are not editorial text.

If a required field is empty, Prepare proof shows a message with a link to the affected field. Correct it and prepare again. Your existing paragraphs and earlier output remain intact. Keeping and downloading are unavailable while the proof has unprepared edits.

If the browser cannot keep a proof, the message says so. You can still edit, prepare and download. If a kept copy is incomplete or unreadable, it does not replace your current work. Forget kept copy removes that unreadable copy when browser storage is available.

06

Know where your proof lives

The desk works locally in your browser. It sends no edited passage to a translation service and publishes nothing. There is no account or shared editorial workspace.

The kept copy belongs to this site in the current browser and origin. A different browser, a different site address, private browsing or cleared site data may not offer it. Browser storage can be denied or full; the success message confirms only that this browser retained the copy when you kept it.

Use Download text for a file outside browser storage. The file contains the complete current prepared proof, not a history of earlier versions. COMMON TONGUE stores one copy; it does not provide cloud backup or version history.

The publication, writer and translator are fictional. The scene, French translation and editorial notes are original material for this site. Human reading and editing remain yours.
