P01

English Original

On the morning we handed over the shop, my father set the key beneath a blue cup, as he had done every morning for thirty-one years. The cup had a chip the shape of a small country. He turned that side towards the wall. Across the street, the baker was lifting trays into the window; each sheet of metal caught the light and sent it briefly into our empty room. We had taken the shelves away the day before. Their pale rectangles were still on the wall.

French Translation

Le matin où nous avons rendu la boutique, mon père a posé la clé sous une tasse bleue, comme il le faisait chaque matin depuis trente et un ans. La tasse avait une ébréchure en forme de petit pays. Il a tourné ce côté vers le mur. De l’autre côté de la rue, le boulanger mettait des plaques dans la vitrine ; chaque feuille de métal attrapait la lumière et l’envoyait un instant dans notre pièce vide. Nous avions enlevé les étagères la veille. Les rectangles plus clairs qu’elles avaient laissés se voyaient encore sur le mur.

P02

English Original

The woman who had rented the room arrived carrying a folding rule and a pear in a paper bag. She would sell frames, she said, though at first she would mostly be making them. My father showed her the socket that worked and the one that only looked as if it did. He explained how the rain came under the door when the wind turned east. She listened without writing anything down. Then she put the pear on the counter and measured the wall.

French Translation

La femme qui avait loué le local est arrivée avec un mètre pliant et une poire dans un sac en papier. Elle vendrait des cadres, a-t-elle dit, mais au début elle passerait surtout son temps à les fabriquer. Mon père lui a montré la prise qui fonctionnait et celle qui en avait seulement l’air. Il lui a expliqué comment la pluie passait sous la porte quand le vent virait à l’est. Elle a écouté sans rien noter. Puis elle a posé la poire sur le comptoir et mesuré le mur.

P03

English Original

I began to tell her where the shelves had stood. My father touched my sleeve, very lightly. She had already seen the rectangles. She opened her rule into a long wooden zigzag, and the room acquired another set of distances. Near the window, she stopped. “The light is good here,” she said. My father looked at the floor, where he had always kept the boxes that were waiting for repair. “Yes,” he said, after a moment. “It is.”

French Translation

Je me suis mis à lui expliquer où se trouvaient les étagères. Mon père a effleuré ma manche. Elle avait déjà vu les rectangles. Elle a déplié son mètre en un long zigzag de bois, et d’autres distances sont apparues dans la pièce. Près de la fenêtre, elle s’est arrêtée. « On a une bonne lumière, ici », a-t-elle dit. Mon père a regardé le sol, là où il avait toujours gardé les boîtes en attente de réparation. « Oui », a-t-il dit après un moment. « C’est vrai. »

P04

English Original

We left the cup for her because neither of us could agree whose it was. Outside, my father put his hand into the pocket where the key should have been. He kept it there as we walked to the corner. The baker’s window was warm against my arm. I asked whether we should buy bread. My father took his hand out of his pocket and counted the coins on his palm, slowly, as if each one had just been returned to him.

French Translation

Nous lui avons laissé la tasse parce que nous n’arrivions pas à décider à qui elle était. Dehors, mon père a glissé la main dans la poche où la clé aurait dû se trouver. Il l’y a gardée jusqu’au coin de la rue. La vitrine du boulanger était chaude contre mon bras. J’ai demandé si nous allions acheter du pain. Mon père a sorti la main de sa poche et a compté les pièces dans sa paume, lentement, comme si on venait de lui rendre chacune d’elles.

After the reading

What moves
between languages

The room is changing hands, but the father’s habits are still there. The French keeps the cup, the pale rectangles and the empty pocket in their original order. In P03, “touched my sleeve, very lightly” becomes “a effleuré ma manche”: the verb holds the lightness without a second phrase. “The room acquired another set of distances” becomes “d’autres distances sont apparues dans la pièce”, keeping the change of perspective without making the room sound like a surveyor. These are editorial choices for this scene; a different reading could make different choices.

Three phrases to linger over

Read a choice in its paragraph before deciding whether you would keep it.

P01
“a chip the shape
of a small country”« une ébréchure en forme de petit pays »

A cup carries a tiny map. Ébréchure keeps the damage at the rim; the country remains small and unnamed.

P03
“touched my sleeve,
very lightly”« a effleuré ma manche »

The French verb puts the restraint into the touch itself. The sentence leaves the father’s reason unstated.

P04
“where the key
should have been”« où la clé aurait dû se trouver »

The missing object is felt through a familiar gesture. The French conditional keeps that expectation in the pocket.
