# Begin design

Make choices that belong to this website. Respect the supplied brand, references, accessibility needs, and accepted direction. Begin's starters illustrate different structures; their appearance is editable.

## Establish a direction

Identify who is visiting, what they need to understand, and their next useful action. Read the actual material and accepted brand choices. Use the subject's work, language, artifacts, or constraints to form a direction. Arrange the content around what the visitor needs to recognize and decide.

Choose actual typefaces and their display, reading and utility roles alongside color, composition and interaction behavior. Match the letterforms to this subject; a reference's supplied font is a starting choice. Explain the few decisions that give this website its character. When the brief leaves an important choice open, compare useful alternatives before choosing; a requested style remains a valid constraint.

For a new substantial direction, use [composition choices](references/composition.md) to compare two or three arrangements of the same content before inheriting a starter's layout. Choose what leads, what shares a surface, and how the next section continues the visitor's decision. Carry that reasoning into the inner task and phone sequence.

For a new direction, a high visual bar, or a composition that remains weak after local polish, use [craft decisions](references/craft.md). Develop the decisive product or content view with real material early, then use it to judge hierarchy, typography and phone order before repeating the design across pages.

Use [direction and composition](references/direction.md) when creating or changing a visual direction. Use [interaction and polish](references/polish.md) for responsive behavior, components, motion, and rendered review. Read only the relevant reference.

When actual letterforms, phrase breaks or visual alignment need deeper study, use [typography decisions](references/typography.md). If different subjects keep inheriting the same family, inspect the relevant [font direction](references/font-directions.md). When the brief leaves the choice open, [choose from its material and task](references/open-choices.md), then review family fit separately from size, wrapping and alignment. For sequences, dependencies or measurements, inspect the relevant [type relationship](references/relational-tasks.md). Carry the useful relationship into the working page and guide.

For a new direction with an open type choice, use [type before the draft](references/before-draft-type.md) to render competing type systems on the real wording before choosing. Keep that preview-informed first source distinct from complete-page refinement. Then inspect consequential names, facts, choices and end actions separately from display headings. [Rendered type choices](references/rendered-type-choices.md) contains earlier complete task comparisons.

For a manual, invitation or prepared artifact whose useful wording loses priority, inspect [reading priority](references/reading-priority.md): complete phrases, sustained prose, generated title/action groups and readable units. Inspect assembled line spacing as well as word grouping.

For an expressive publication, graphic club or numeric tool, inspect the relevant [typography transfer](references/typography-transfers.md). Complete source and native openings show roles, readable conditions and useful anchors across a home, task and guide. Check the new content and consequential states before adopting the relationship.

When transferring a type direction to a new task, use [type in new tasks](references/type-in-new-tasks.md) for meaningful groups, visible italic/roman boundaries, populated numeric anchors and editable prose. Its complete theatre, ceramics and household examples show conditional choices across the site and guide.

When a new task keeps falling into the same visual family, use [contrasting tasks](references/contrasting-tasks.md). Its night programme, object mosaic and operating sheet show distinct material relationships, wider phone reading, selected-work access and useful completion actions. Their shared font pair is a limited choice, not a font-selection benchmark.

For reference-led work or limited assets, read [worked decisions](references/decisions.md). Extract a decision that fits the new brief and implement an original response. A screenshot establishes visible composition; interaction, conversion, mobile behavior, and internal implementation need their own evidence.

For a service with few assets, a photography-led site, or a dense business workspace, use the relevant [material and density decisions](references/material-and-density.md). Compose the useful offer, image sequence or record relationship with the actual content; inspect the inner task and phone arrangement as well as the opening.

For a small publication whose main action is reading, use the [Fieldnotes editorial case](references/fieldnotes.md): view its desktop and phone captures, then inspect the named HTML/CSS if adapting its implementation. A service, product, or other brief should use a reference that fits its own audience, material, and action. Fieldnotes is one optional case.

For a product whose value depends on work, versions, feedback or decisions, inspect the relevant [Reviewroom product case](references/reviewroom.md). It includes two original compositions, matching working pages and captures. Choose the useful relationship for the new task; the example's brand, wording and local review flow are editable material.

For record inspection, activity planning, a spatial study, a professional service, a photographic stay, or a production queue, use the relevant [task case](references/task-cases.md). Compare its actual working page and guide as well as its public opening. Transfer the useful relationships with the new subject's material and required behavior.

For a book catalogue, listening evening or study-week utility, inspect the relevant [reference transfer](references/transfer-cases.md). Each gives the actual source, useful task, guide and phone/desktop openings. Follow the material relationship that helps the new brief; the example’s local state model is specific to its task.

## Build and refine

Use actual content to test the composition. A long name, a missing image, or a second paragraph should not break the layout. Align headings, text, controls, and media deliberately. Repeated cards suit genuinely comparable items; choose another structure when the content has a sequence or an uneven hierarchy.

Inspect a real preview when available. Review reading order, text measure, contrast, mobile layout, focus, and the central action before adding decoration. Motion should make a state change understandable or support a specific moment. Respect reduced motion.

Leave the direction understandable in the current project record or design artifact: audience/action, composition, type/color roles, useful reference decisions, and unresolved choices. Keep this proportionate to the task.

When the person asks for a change, identify its intended effect and preserve the direction they already accepted. If the existing direction is causing a concrete problem, explain the problem and a focused alternative. Separate measured failures from aesthetic judgments; do not call the site visually reviewed if only source code was read.
