# Craft decisions that change the result

Use this when defining a substantial visual direction or when tidy details still leave the page unconvincing. Apply the parts that resolve the actual brief. An accepted direction or a small repair does not need a new concept study.

## Find the product's own evidence

Identify the object the visitor cares about: a file awaiting review, a draft becoming a planned post, a lesson that makes a difficult step clear, or a photograph with a story. Compose around that material. A generic benefit headline and an empty dashboard do not explain what this product helps the person do.

For a product, name one capability that changes the task. Show its input, useful change and resulting state. Test whether a visitor could explain the benefit from that view. If the same view could advertise a simple list or an unrelated competitor equally well, reveal the distinctive operation: a comment attached to its version, a decision with its reasons, or a draft whose edited text appears in the planned result. Use actual supported behavior or an accurately labeled sample.

For a publication, the decisive view may be a cover with its real story and the first useful passage. For a service, it may be an annotated piece of work that demonstrates how the owner thinks. A product interface is appropriate only when it explains the subject.

## Study the decisive view before multiplying it

Build the opening or working detail that carries this evidence using the actual headline, body copy, labels and required content density. Set type roles, line breaks, surface proportions, alignment and spacing together. For a new substantial direction, use [composition choices](composition.md) to compare two or three compact arrangements before inheriting a starter's layout. Keep the material fixed and change what leads, what spans the width, what shares a surface, and what follows. Choose by what becomes easier to understand and act on.

Inspect a real desktop and phone rendering when possible. On the phone, judge where identity, offer, useful material and action appear against this visitor's task. The work or first instruction may deserve to lead. Repeated navigation, badges and introductory copy spend space before the work. Keep controls the task needs, and group or shorten supporting context when it delays recognition. A tall image may be useful; shrinking every element to force one screen usually damages reading.

Read the display text at its final line length and the body text as several consecutive paragraphs. Check whether the typography carries the intended character while sustaining reading. The longest meaningful title, a selected state and a full feedback paragraph should fit the design. This view supplies evidence for extending the direction to the remaining pages; a token list alone does not.

When the type direction is unresolved, compare plausible letterforms in the same decisive view. Keep the wording and imagery fixed, and judge the whole relationship after tuning each candidate.

Give each text role a reason to exist. A title need not have a final full stop or an accompanying subtitle. Inspect model-added eyebrows and introductory lines: retain them when they supply needed context, remove them when the title or actual content already does that work. Recheck spacing and wrapping after removal while preserving instructions, conditions and the accepted voice.

Read the full page as a sequence. Check the transition from the opening into useful evidence, the different weights of offer, work and explanation, and the point where the visitor can act. Then inspect the linked task or reading page. Keep its identity coherent while composing around the work people do there. Repeating an opening's arrangement across every section can give unequal information the same emphasis.

If no actual rendered view is available, state that the composition remains unreviewed. Supplied captures can support a composition judgment; inspecting controls and movement requires their own evidence. Source inspection can resolve implementation questions but cannot establish how the page feels.

## Let inner pages serve their work

In an editor, distinguish the person's artifact from interface labels and actions. When reading and editing the same document, preserve its title and body roles across the fields and preview while keeping status and controls clear. Name the preview's purpose and whether it shows current input, saved work or a prepared result. Dense records and numeric tools need their own content roles; a serif writing treatment is one possible response, not a default for every editor.

Desktop choices can show useful summaries beside the work when people need that context to choose. On a phone, consider compact choices that bring the selected work and its controls earlier. Keep the identity and meaningful selection context while changing their arrangement.

Before a consequential action, show the information needed to decide: the chosen session, quantity and total, or the work included in a prepared result. Check their actual phone order beside the action. Repeated large illustrations and homepage introductions can delay these controls. Keep the reading and keyboard order coherent when rearranging them.

After a preparation action, keep its result near the action in the reading sequence. A complete static reference can sit after the working result or in a useful disclosure. Compose the unprepared state with one clear instruction; reserve substantial artifact space and emphasis for useful populated work.

An annotated drawing needs a responsive composition of its own. Shrinking the plan, internal legend and margins together can make every label unreadable despite fitting the screen. Give the useful drawing enough space, move explanations into readable nearby text when appropriate, and preserve the full original and the selected layers' meaning.

Inspect intrinsic image dimensions and nested reading columns at the actual phone width. A retained fixed image height can create a tall empty field around a landscape drawing. A narrow text column beside a small sketch can fit while making reading tiring. Resolve the affected relationship and inspect its breakpoint neighbors.

For a short guide, read the opening instruction and the next few steps as one continuous passage. On a wide screen, the introduction can share the instruction column while the title or contents sits beside it. On a phone, useful navigation should help reach the passage without becoming another long introduction. Keep routine expected results as concise nearby cues; give a consequential condition enough prominence to affect the reader's decision.

Begin a walkthrough with its useful outcome and direct task entry. Make cleanup or reset conditional when the current state already lets the person begin; explain what existing work would change. Retain practical save, edit and recovery distinctions near their steps. Put developer field names and record structures in a subordinate reference or appendix when readers only need ordinary labels. Preserve a compact route to the relevant help on phone and useful navigation through a longer manual.

## Transfer a reference decision with its conditions

View the relevant reference at a useful size, then inspect matching source when available. Extract the relationship that explains the task: what leads, what sits beside it, how emphasis changes between sections, and what moves first on a phone. Note the content and imagery that make it work.

Choose one principal composition. Borrow a second reference for a distinct job, such as a legible guide or a useful state transition, when it fits the same identity. Replacing the original subject requires reconsidering image proportion, copy length and content order; a new palette does not complete that adaptation.

Keep stylistic constraints local to the chosen direction. Serif type, warm surfaces, darkness, dense layouts and generous space can each be appropriate. Preserve the owner's brand. A source's taste defaults do not become rules for every Begin website.

## Keep the same work coherent through a change

When an example or real interface changes state, keep its title, version, content, feedback and status attached to the same object. A selection change updates the preview and related labels together. A changed status updates its text, color, icon and accessible state. An edited draft must appear in the resulting view; reset and reload should restore the state described by the interface.

For an interactive composition, inspect at least the consequential states it actually needs: before the action, the changed result, and a useful correction or empty state. This is part of the visual design because stale color, mismatched content or a displaced control can make a polished interface misleading.

When a status label grows after an edit, inspect it inside the narrow item control. A page can have no viewport overflow while its longer badge covers the neighboring item's content. Check the affected relationship with the actual changed label.

## Choreograph a useful transition

Use movement to explain what changed: an item enters the selected area, feedback appears beside the work, or an edited draft becomes planned. Keep a few curves and durations appropriate to that identity; choose a transition for the relationship it communicates.

Read the static view first, then inspect the actual transition separately. Text swaps need their own timing so old and new labels do not overlap. At the moment of greatest density, the artifact and decision should remain legible. Finish in a clear stable state, preserve keyboard focus, and provide the same task with reduced motion. A recorded demonstration and a working webpage require separate checks.

## Retain what helped

Record the adopted reference decision, useful content constraints and relevant resource paths in the project's existing note. After a supported revision, keep the visible problem, change and observed result concise. A worked example teaches the conditions under which a decision helped; use it to inform a new brief rather than copying every feature or repeating its process automatically.
