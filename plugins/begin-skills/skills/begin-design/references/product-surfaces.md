# From product explanation to useful work

Use this when a public product page leads into an application, configurator or guided task. Start with the actual person, material and operation. Choose typography and composition for that subject; these relationships do not prescribe a visual style.

## Let one example explain a complete action

Choose a meaningful example with enough real detail to explain the product. If a preview invites someone to configure, inspect or revise that example, carry its identity and relevant values into the working surface. The person should recognize what they saw and be able to perform the promised action. A decorative product window or an unrelated default record breaks this connection.

A selected example can change the visual material, explanation and related facts together. Keep the example's identity visible through the change. Use actual sample content rather than invented endorsements or impressive totals that the implementation cannot support. Explain local demonstration scope where someone could mistake a prepared result for a real transaction.

## Give a working surface an information contract

Decide what must stay understandable while the person changes a filter, a selected item or an input: the current object, the scope of the visible collection, consequential facts and the next useful action. A table, focused editor or step sequence can each satisfy that contract. Do not inherit an application sidebar or dashboard card grid without a task that needs it.

Check the same contract on a phone. A smaller table or a stack of cards can conceal which item the details belong to. An attractive isolated component does not establish a useful complete workspace. Render a representative populated state, including longer identifiers and the actual result, before repeating the frame across the application.

When two states can be true together, give each its own visible treatment. A time can already belong to a request and also be the current proposed selection. Keep the saved entry recognizable while showing the proposed range independently; an accessible selected state alone does not help someone comparing the visible timetable. Check the unchanged original and the proposed edit together before confirmation.

Put consequential facts where they are used. A machine, date and time shown above a long grid may be out of view when someone reaches Add. Repeat a compact current-session identity near that action when it resolves this distance. For subtitle review, complete cue boundaries need comfortable reading even though they look like metadata. Allow labeled time groups or a current/total heading to take separate lines before reducing them to decorative type. Choose the size and arrangement from the actual words and frame, rather than carrying one numeric setting into every product.

## Keep a useful route through long work

After selecting an item on a phone, reveal the next editing step or give a nearby continuation. Move focus to a visible destination; focusing an offscreen field with scrolling prevented leaves the operation unclear. Keep the current item's identity with that destination. The appropriate response can be a jump, a shorter sequence or a different arrangement; a large useful collection need not become a small one.

If a final panel names known items that block completion, let those items lead back to the work. A real cue button can select the corresponding cue, reveal its editor and give its heading or first useful control focus. Preserve the current clip and review state, then provide a route back to the whole revision. Make the clickable role and reviewed/unchecked distinction visible, and exercise pointer and keyboard routes separately.

A retained preview can make a physical object, selected parts and price easier to inspect. Check its space cost against that benefit. When keeping one, follow the backward keyboard route from prepared output as well as the forward action: the focused control and its full focus indicator must remain visible below the actual retained surface. An anchor offset does not protect ordinary Tab or Shift+Tab focus. This is a condition for keeping such a preview, not a reason to add one to unrelated tasks.

## Make dependencies visible

When a later result depends on earlier choices, changing those choices must recalculate it or clearly make it stale. Keep valid input when going back. Before the final action, show the exact object, material values and consequence being confirmed. A highlighted progress step and a reassuring success animation do not establish that the underlying operation happened.

For reviewed work, attach approval to the exact revision or content basis. A text or timing change must not carry an earlier approved label into the new export. For saved work, distinguish the current working copy from a deliberate checkpoint. If Restore can replace current work, show whether a valid checkpoint exists and the useful saved metadata after reload or a guide visit, rather than only immediately after Save.

Scope completion language to the actual capability: a local saved draft, a prepared file, an exported request and an accepted server operation are different outcomes. A retry or cancellation must leave an understandable result. A preview's inert button is evidence about presentation only; an original complete example needs a usable destination or an honest explanation of its boundary.

## Inspect the whole journey

Review the public promise, the entered task, a changed populated state, the result and the route back. Judge complete typography and content at those points as well as the opening. Keep screenshots and actual operations as separate evidence. A preloader or continuous effect must not prevent access to the explanation or task when nonessential media is slow.

Browser drafts are tied to the serving origin and profile: separate projects sharing an origin need distinct keys. Use only sample information; real persistence, access control and external actions require their own implementation and evidence.

## Evidence and limits

The three makers compared two type systems on actual wording at desktop and phone widths, then froze complete first sources before independent whole-site criticism. The first reviews exercised each public example and core local data paths, including actual JSON/HTML, CSV and SRT downloads. They also found consequential defects: LIFT's backward focus could sit behind its retained preview; BENCHTIME masked the current selection and lost useful phone/session and checkpoint context; CUTLINE made cue times too small, split a cue-count group and reported blockers without a direct route back. These findings support inspecting the complete operation, not a claim that a type comparison guarantees a finished product.

Independent follow-up observed the repaired routes. LIFT's 320px backward focus stays visible; checkpoint/guide/reload recovery and actual JSON/HTML readback agree. BENCHTIME shows the proposed range separately from the original request, brings the phone editor into view, keeps session identity near Add and reads checkpoint status after navigation. Its actual two-session CSV preserves Unicode text, Dublin time and the €42 total. CUTLINE's narrow time groups and cue identity remain readable; pointer and keyboard review items reach the matching cue. A time-only edit invalidates approval, and the newly reviewed SRT contains that exact revision. Checkpoint restore and reload retain its approved version. [CHECKS.json](../assets/product-surface-cases/CHECKS.json) records the inspected paths and limits; [PROVENANCE.json](../assets/product-surface-cases/PROVENANCE.json) binds the curated source and captures to their evidence. These observed outcomes remain distinct from source inspection.

Release review also repaired two input-identity guards. Unknown URL keys must select only explicitly supported examples or clips; JavaScript's inherited object properties are not valid product identities. Native browser checks confirmed that those inputs leave usable editors and ordinary configuration names, while normal sample selection and edited-clip recovery remain intact. Separate source freezes record the guards; the preserved opening captures still belong to their original rendering.

The retained specimens and complete cases demonstrate bounded internal trials. They establish neither user acceptance nor reliable first-draft quality across hosts or product types. Ordinary storage recovery does not prove denied/full/corrupt-storage behavior, and a reduced-motion CSS rule does not establish a runtime preference test. Recheck the conditions used by the new task. Preserve the case notices and font licenses; see [NOTICES.md](../assets/product-surface-cases/NOTICES.md).
