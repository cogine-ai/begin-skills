# Choose the content's spatial relationship

Use this for a new substantial direction or a page whose structure still obscures its subject after local refinement. Respect an accepted composition during small edits. Start with the visitor's decision, actual material, and useful action.

## Compare the same material

Before inheriting a starter's layout, compare two or three compact arrangements using the same real headline, explanation, artifact or images, and action. Sketches or small working views are enough to begin. Change which element leads, which spans the width, which shares a surface, and what follows into the next section. Choose the arrangement whose emphasis explains the subject and helps the visitor act. Inspect the chosen arrangement rendered on phone and desktop before extending it.

## Eight useful relationships

These are conditional choices. A page may use different relationships for different jobs while retaining one identity.

| Relationship | When it helps | Compose and judge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Statement above broad evidence** | A product view, drawing or result needs width to be understood | Keep the statement and action compact, then give the actual artifact room. Choose an inspectable state. Can the visitor recognize the operation without reading tiny labels? On phone, select or recompose the useful detail. |
| **Work leads, explanation follows** | Strong owned work can establish the subject quickly | Let the artifact take first prominence; keep its identity, caption and next action close. Follow with the explanation it needs. Does the visitor understand what the work represents and whose work it is? |
| **One image forms the scene** | An appropriate photograph carries place, people or character | Compose a short message around the image's useful subject. Give text a readable region or solid surface. Check the actual desktop and phone crops, text contrast, and the next practical facts. |
| **A typographic field** | Few images are available and the offer has concrete language | Use scale, alignment, rules and a readable service sequence to establish identity. Keep scope and action within the useful reading path. Does the title's size help recognition while leaving room for what someone can buy or do? |
| **A work sequence or gallery** | Several artifacts supply the value | Choose a featured item, chronological sequence or comparable index from the actual content. Let priority determine space. Keep captions and destinations intelligible. Does each item's weight reflect what it can explain? |
| **The action leads** | One input or choice reveals the value | Give the input, needed context and understandable result the principal space. Keep examples and limitations accurate. Can visitors understand the action before taking it, then recognize its result and correct their input? |
| **A shared stage with overlap** | Related artwork or product views form one meaningful scene | Let edges overlap where they explain a relationship, while preserving a clear reading and action area. Keep objects identifiable and text legible. Recompose or separate them on phone when overlap obscures meaning. |
| **Adjacent explanation and evidence** | Visitors need two complementary regions in view together | Choose unequal widths and alignment from their content. Keep the artifact readable and its explanation near the relevant detail. Does the adjacency help comparison or recognition? Reconsider the phone order instead of automatically stacking copy first. |

## Carry the choice through the page

Write a short sequence in the current project note: what leads, what supplies evidence, what helps a decision, and where the visitor acts. Give each following section its own job and proportion. A broad work view can lead into a narrow explanation, a ruled service sequence, or a compact action area. The transition should advance understanding.

Compose the inner task around its work. An editor needs current content and controls; an enquiry needs scope, inputs and the current result; a guide needs useful instructions and expected outcomes. Keep identity and type roles coherent while changing arrangements for those jobs.

When a work item promises a series, system or sequence, its detail should let visitors inspect that promise. Use real applications or meaningful variations with concise captions explaining what changes. Keep those artifacts attached to the correct study. A larger copy of the cover alone may leave the visitor with little new evidence. Provide a clear route from study entry to selecting or briefing, while keeping navigation truthful to the displayed section.

Inspect the phone sequence as its own composition. Keep the useful artifact, needed explanation and action in a meaningful order. A crop may need a separate image, an annotated drawing may need nearby HTML labels, and a collection may need a compact selector. Preserve reading and keyboard order.

Check what the reference relationship becomes with the new material. A work wall can become a book shelf, but a publisher also needs an interior sample that helps someone choose. A broad document can become a week plan, but its dates and sessions need readable HTML and a useful phone agenda. A photograph and text on one background still need a deliberate spatial relationship; a shared color alone can leave two unrelated halves. Keep subjects, ownership and captions accurate when a photograph illustrates a fictional offer.

For a collection, give the featured item first attention without making visitors traverse the same large item again in the index. Keep a compact route to the remaining choices. Give the selected item's decision material and its action appropriate space; a secondary collection action should bring the person's collection into view.

## Review the whole result

- Name the dominant element and the next point of attention. Explain how the quiet space supports them.
- Read the real title, supporting copy and first useful evidence together. Remove repeated context when it delays recognition.
- Inspect the opening's transition and the full page. Check whether important and supporting information have appropriate weight.
- Follow the central action into its destination or changed state. Preserve the same artifact, identity and values.
- Read the inner task or passage on phone and desktop. Check long text, image crops, labels, focus and the context before controls.
- Separate measured defects from aesthetic judgment. A build or overflow check supports its own result; composition quality needs a rendered review.

Record the selected relationship, why it fits the material, and the relevant observed checks. Keep an initial version before critique when evaluating a new method's effect.
