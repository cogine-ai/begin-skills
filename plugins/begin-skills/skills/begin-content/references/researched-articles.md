# Write an article that answers a real question

Use this for researched explanations, comparisons, practical articles, and supported opinion pieces. The deliverable is a complete answer for the intended reader. A promotional ending is optional and should fit the article's purpose.

## Establish the brief

Identify the reader, their central question, what they already know, and what the article should add. Use the supplied length and format when given; otherwise choose enough detail to answer the question. Read existing owner material and accepted positions. A short brief can record the question, scope, intended contribution, useful sources, and unresolved facts.

Choose the article's job: explain a mechanism, compare choices, investigate a question, or recommend a course of action. Keep background proportional. Avoid choosing a trendy topic only to fill an empty blog.

## Research the claims

Identify which claims need external support before drafting. Prefer original research, official documentation, original records, and the relevant organisation's own statements for its policies or products. A first-party source can still be selective or promotional. Inspect its methods, scope, date/version, and relevance; use independent evidence when the question requires it.

Open the actual material. Search snippets and generated summaries help discovery but do not establish the underlying claim. Follow citations to the original evidence when practical. Check changing information close to publication. If access fails, narrow the claim, use available reliable evidence, or identify the missing source; do not imply the source was inspected.

For a research-heavy piece, keep a short claim/source record:

| Claim | Support and location | Limits or editorial treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What the source establishes | Exact page/file and relevant passage or result | Population, setting, date, uncertainty, or author interest |
| What the owner experienced | Supplied account or original material | Attribute it; do not generalise it into a measured industry result |
| What the article concludes | Reasoning connecting the evidence | Identify an inference or recommendation where relevant |

Several articles repeating one study do not supply several independent studies. Citation volume does not repair weak support. Describe disagreement fairly and retain the uncertainty that affects the answer.

## Build an answer and its reasoning

State the central question or answer early. Organise sections around what the reader must understand, rather than the order in which sources were found. Explain unfamiliar terms where useful.

For each material claim, connect the evidence to the conclusion. Say why the fact matters, which assumption the recommendation needs, and when an alternative would be reasonable. A comparison should use criteria that matter to the reader. An exploratory piece can reach a qualified conclusion; it need not manufacture a debate or a decisive winner.

Add original explanation, a useful worked example, or a decision aid. Clearly label hypothetical scenarios. Do not rewrite a source paragraph by paragraph or copy its expressive language. Use only short necessary quotations, attribute them, and respect the applicable quotation and licence limits. Paraphrased evidence still needs attribution.

## Draft in the owner's voice

Write clear English with useful headings and enough context to follow the argument. Keep a distinction between sourced facts, interpretation, and advice through attribution and natural phrasing. Do not imply the owner conducted research, tested products, served clients, or holds qualifications that the supplied material does not establish.

Place source links near the claims they support. A bibliography can supplement those links. Match the title and opening to the actual scope; avoid promising a complete answer when a material part remains unknown. Conclude with the answer, its useful conditions, or a next step for learning or action. A sales invitation is appropriate only when it serves the brief.

## Revise and prepare for publication

Review the article's meaning first: does it answer the question, add something useful, and explain its reasoning? Remove unsupported leaps and sections that repeat a point. Then verify material claims, source links, names, quotations, numbers, dates, and attribution. Preserve important qualifications.

Edit for reading flow, stable vocabulary, concrete examples, and the owner's voice. Check the published format when available: headings, narrow-screen reading, linked references, captions, and accessible media. Keep the article useful as text when an optional image is absent.

Prepare an accurate title, summary, author attribution, and publication/update information according to the site's conventions. Mark placeholders and unresolved claims in the draft; replace or resolve them before representing it as finished. Record what would require a future update. Do not change a date simply to suggest new work.

Finish with the article and a short editorial note identifying sources checked, material recommendations or uncertainty, and outstanding publication requirements. Do not claim it was published or that readers benefited unless those outcomes were observed.

Primary materials inspected on 2026-10-09:

- [GOV.UK: Identify user needs](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/plan-manage-content/identify-user-needs/): readers and existing evidence; adapted beyond its government-service setting.
