# Turn rough material into a content plan

Use this when the owner has notes, a few existing pages, a transcript, scattered images, or more ideas than a clear structure. Produce enough of a plan to start useful writing. The owner should not need to complete a marketing questionnaire first.

## Read what exists

Identify the intended readers and their questions. Inspect the actual material, its author, intended use, accepted wording, and any relevant date. Distinguish:

- facts the owner can stand behind;
- the owner's opinions, voice, and examples;
- public source material requiring verification;
- old, duplicated, contradictory, or incomplete content;
- gaps that prevent a meaningful answer.

Keep a compact inventory when material is scattered: item, what it contributes, intended surface, and issue to resolve. Do not turn draft notes into customer claims or treat a proposed feature as available. A transcript supplies what the speaker said; it does not independently establish every claim.

## Give the content a purpose

Write one reader question or desired result for each proposed page or piece. A service page explains fit and collaboration. An article examines a useful question. A procedure helps someone complete a task. Interface text explains an action or state. These require different structures even when they concern the same product.

Choose where a point belongs before polishing it. Repeated questions may need one clear answer linked from relevant pages. A long supporting explanation may deserve an article rather than extending the introduction. Do not add pages simply to make the website look larger.

For a small task, propose a short list. For several pieces, a table can identify the reader/question, surface, useful material, missing facts, and first draft priority. Prioritize what a visitor needs now and what the owner can maintain. Search or traffic data can inform that choice when available; do not invent demand estimates.

## Worked planning decision

**Supplied brief:** an independent website consultant has rough service notes, a sample project checklist, and a question often raised in supplied enquiries: whether a first website needs a blog. The materials contain no customer outcomes or published case studies.

| Material | Decision | Needed before final copy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service notes explain deliverables and owner responsibilities | Build a service page around fit, scope, and how collaboration works | Confirm what is included and how someone contacts the owner |
| Draft says “websites that double your leads” | Remove the unsupported result; describe the actual work | Retain a quantified claim only if the owner provides applicable support |
| Original project checklist | Adapt into a preparation guide with meaningful steps | Confirm the checklist matches the real process |
| Blog question from supplied enquiries | Develop an article comparing useful reasons to publish with the maintenance involved | Inspect primary guidance and keep the consultant's recommendations distinct |

This is a fictional planning example. It supplies no evidence about market demand or a real consultant's customers.

## Write and maintain

Start the highest-priority piece with the material already available. Preserve meaningful owner language, replace vague promises with actual substance, and ask for the few missing details that change the answer. Keep unresolved facts visible in the working draft rather than spreading placeholders throughout finished copy.

Record what was accepted, what remains unresolved, and who can confirm it in the existing project record or content artifact. Use the project's current publishing format and content ownership conventions. A content plan is not a publishing system: scheduling, CMS configuration, and distribution need their own actual implementation when requested.

**Method background:** [GOV.UK's user-needs guidance](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/plan-manage-content/identify-user-needs/) connects content with readers' tasks and existing evidence. Its public-service context does not require every commercial website to use the same template. The inventory, planning example, and recommendations above are original methods for this pack.
