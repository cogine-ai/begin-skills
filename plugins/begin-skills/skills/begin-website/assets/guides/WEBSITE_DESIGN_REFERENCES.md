# Website design references

Research date: 2026-10-08. Sources: Mobbin and Refero. This is a working selection for the next Begin design iteration. The existing three starters were created before this research; the references below do not describe their original provenance. The 2026-10-09 edition adds original worked design decisions from the research; starter redesigns remain future work.

The dev.4 candidate also includes the original [Fieldnotes worked case](../../../begin-design/references/fieldnotes.md), with actual current phone/desktop captures, matching implementation and asset provenance. Its accepted local homepage and subsequent transfer study support this scoped example. The external shortlist below remains research rather than bundled third-party assets or a universal style prescription.

## How to use these references

Choose references after identifying the visitor, the website's purpose, the available content, and the owner's preferred character. Explain which decision each reference informs. A composition can be useful even when its typography, imagery, or interaction is unsuitable for the new site.

The observations below describe inspected screenshots or explicitly identified source text. They do not establish conversion performance, mobile behavior, accessibility compliance, loading speed, or working interactions. Recheck those properties in the implementation. The external cases below are links and independently written analysis; their screenshots, website code, and branding assets are not bundled. The original Fieldnotes case includes its separately licensed fonts with notices.

### Adding a bundled case

Curate each included case around a visitor task. Supply:

- Its audience, central action, suitable uses, and important limits.
- Actual phone and desktop captures paired with the exact editable implementation and capture sizes.
- The few useful design decisions and the source locations that implement them.
- Complete important destinations and honest content, asset provenance, and applicable notices.
- Actual observations for reading, navigation, and the main action; state anything not checked.
- A small fresh adaptation trial when evaluating reuse, recording which resources were read and what survived the new content.

Record whose judgment supports the selection. An owner's approval, a curator's preference, and visitor results are different observations. External research can inform an original case; include external assets or code only with applicable reuse rights. Choose cases that add a useful task or design decision, and load only the case relevant to the current work.

## Mobbin: inspected website screens and sections

Six searches returned 26 screenshots: product homepages (5), service introductions (5), portfolio introductions (5), selected work (5), service process (3), and pricing comparisons (3). All returned previews were examined. The focused selection follows.

### M01 — YLLW: a service visitors can recognize

- Evidence: [service introduction](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/ce502cd9-c17f-45be-ad46-a91198465bdd), [process introduction](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/fa9788f2-54fc-4579-9173-7052eadacd20).
- Observed: a tall photograph of a person at work sits beside a compact project-management heading, explanatory paragraph, and meeting action. The process introduction uses a large, condensed multiline heading above appointment and service links.
- Transfer: make the service tangible through an owned image and a concrete explanation of the work. Keep the action adjacent to the explanation. Give the process a separate role in the page.
- Fit: consultants, independent professionals, small service businesses.
- Constraint: the photographed scene supplies much of the credibility. Replacing it with an unrelated abstract decoration changes the result. The process screenshot shows an introduction, not the four individual steps.

### M02 — COLLINS: concentration around a main statement

- Evidence: [homepage introduction](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/76aeaeb4-d741-43d4-a7c6-65dbbd902701).
- Observed: a short centered serif headline occupies a large white field. A small group of award labels sits below; a colorful project image enters at the bottom of the frame. Header navigation has very little visual weight.
- Transfer: a clear statement and deliberate space can establish hierarchy before the work appears. Keep supporting evidence quieter than the principal message.
- Fit: a studio or professional with a meaningful central proposition and strong supporting work.
- Constraint: this restraint depends on good content. A generic statement in the same layout still gives the visitor little information. Awards must come from actual records.

### M03 — Fiasco: projects with visual rhythm and context

- Evidence: [selected work](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/4ed7c118-a866-41fa-824c-32a24323c927), [process section](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/628d4c6b-cea8-49f0-88ca-353161a59678).
- Observed: the work section has two columns with unequal image widths and staggered vertical positions. Each project has a name, a short explanation, and small category labels. The process section places a numbered stage list at the left, an explanation near the center, and scattered work photographs around it.
- Transfer: retain a consistent information contract for each project while varying image proportions. Explain what a project represents; let a process section reveal how the work happens.
- Fit: designers, creative studios, consultants with case studies.
- Constraint: a screenshot does not prove how the numbered process list responds. Establish mobile reading order before adopting the staggered composition.

### M04 — MOUTHWASH Studio: hierarchy through project scale

- Evidence: [selected work](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/e2bc218a-3568-43c0-a95e-b95b0d829e6c).
- Observed: a bold section label introduces two adjacent project images. One tall red image receives substantially more space than the neighboring architectural image. Small project captions sit beneath the images.
- Transfer: choose a featured project and give it deliberate visual weight. Keep the interface quiet enough for the work to define the palette.
- Fit: a portfolio with strong owned images and one or two projects that deserve priority.
- Constraint: arbitrary image sizes can imply a priority the owner did not intend. Choose cropping and emphasis from the content.

### M05 — Mixpanel: explain the product with the product

- Evidence: [product homepage](https://mobbin.com/screens/370972da-101d-4bfc-930f-aecb80c9e87f).
- Observed: a centered heading and two actions lead into customer logos and a broad analytics interface. Several interface panels overlap, while the surrounding gradient remains secondary to the charts and controls.
- Transfer: put a meaningful product view near the promise. Select a recognizable task or result instead of filling the product window with generic decorative bars.
- Fit: software with an interface that explains its value visually.
- Constraint: customer logos need permission and factual support. Cropped product panels still need a clear story; adding more panels can reduce understanding.

### M06 — Remote: separate the explanation from the demonstration

- Evidence: [product homepage](https://mobbin.com/screens/b6423c2e-0877-4e18-8c28-80fc2be490b6).
- Observed: the left side of a wide opening panel contains the headline, paragraph, and two actions. The right side contains a collage of product interfaces. A separate trust row follows the panel, then the next feature section begins.
- Transfer: give copy, demonstration, and supporting evidence distinct positions. The visitor should be able to follow the offer before inspecting interface detail.
- Fit: a product whose workflow can be demonstrated in a small number of recognizable views.
- Constraint: small interface labels in a screenshot do not prove readability on a phone. The reconstruction needs a mobile composition and realistic product content.

### M07 — Equals: distinguish plans at a glance

- Evidence: [pricing section](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/54c59322-41f7-4850-8426-73d076f881ce).
- Observed: three pricing columns share a structure. Saturated blue, purple, and black headers distinguish the plans, followed by prices, seat information, and feature lists.
- Transfer: give comparable plans a consistent information order and clear visual separation. State the audience or intended use of each plan.
- Fit: a product with a few meaningful purchase choices.
- Constraint: screenshot prices are historical examples. Set terms from the new product's business model; check color contrast and whether visitors actually need three plans.

### M08 — incident.io: detailed comparison as a separate decision aid

- Evidence: [plan comparison](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/a6fc8b97-6f8c-4280-b8db-5bafdfa236d9).
- Observed: a feature table aligns four plan columns. Section labels group the rows. A second strip of plan names and actions is visible along the bottom of the captured view.
- Transfer: use a comparison table when visitors need to resolve specific feature differences. Keep plan identity and the next action close to the comparison.
- Fit: buyers evaluating material limits or capabilities across plans.
- Constraint: the screenshot does not establish that the bottom strip is sticky. A small first-release product may need a simpler pricing explanation.

### M09 — Locomotive: an expressive alternative

- Evidence: [agency introduction](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/6a2f519f-af72-4525-a6ca-2ef3457bdf63).
- Observed: a saturated red photographic portrait occupies the opening view. Small navigation and an agency label sit over the image; the brand name and serif service description appear near its lower edge.
- Transfer: a strong owned image can supply the website's character while a restrained interface gives it room.
- Fit: creative professionals who intentionally want an expressive introduction.
- Constraint: text over an image needs a tested contrast treatment. This reference does not establish video, animation, or performance behavior.

### Screening notes

- The first portfolio search returned blank or insufficiently rendered introductions for Metalab, Aino Agency, and Unseen Studio. Those frames were excluded from visual recommendations. A later Unseen Studio selected-work result was readable, but other cases supplied a more useful first shortlist.
- The Semrush result was a tool home screen with an input and application sidebar, so it was excluded from the marketing-site shortlist.
- Other readable results remain discovery candidates: Origin, Koto, basement.studio, Squarespace, StackAI, Zoho, Cloudstudio, Tines, and Retool. Query matches were checked against actual content; a service page or process introduction was not relabeled as a complete homepage or flow.

## Refero: public case review

Five primary cases and one limited supplementary capture were visually inspected through the official [Refero Styles library](https://styles.refero.design/). The evidence is Refero's recorded desktop screenshots. Live original sites, motion, mobile layouts, and interactions were not tested. Case links remain the durable discovery pointers; the source screenshots are not bundled.

### R01 — Grafik: the work supplies the visual identity

- Evidence: [Refero case](https://styles.refero.design/style/0226e028-3cd3-440d-b469-ca459267161d).
- Observed: text navigation, a tight three-column wall of projects, and thin dividing lines. Device-framed websites alternate with portrait photography. The subdued interface allows colors inside the work to carry the composition.
- Transfer: decide the sequence, proportions, and grouping of project images before adding decorative interface elements. Rich work can support a very restrained frame.
- Fit: a designer or studio with strong owned work.
- Constraint: a grid cannot supply missing project quality or context. Add the creator's role, project explanation, and a useful contact route when visitors need those details.

### R02 — Aspelin Reitan: a real scene carries the character

- Evidence: [Refero case](https://styles.refero.design/style/3a331157-f5a5-4640-ada8-8a3ad262ee6a).
- Observed: architectural photography fills the opening view. A wordmark, outlined portfolio control, and menu sit near the top; a short two-line question sits near the lower left. Building curves, materials, and the city scene organize the image.
- Transfer: let an owned result or workplace scene explain the character of a service. Compose a short message around the image; a large headline is only one possible way to establish hierarchy.
- Fit: architecture, spaces, hospitality, or services with useful photographic results.
- Constraint: the approach needs a credible image and direct business context. Test text readability with different crops; visitors who need an immediate service explanation must receive it.

### R03 — Régis Grumberg: typography as personal expression

- Evidence: [Refero case](https://styles.refero.design/style/1a2ca4fb-1087-4fd0-83ba-590bc63f54ee).
- Observed: three rings of white text surround a central entry control on a black canvas. The name, experimental portfolio label, and personal statement make the graphic composition; there is very little surrounding interface.
- Transfer: an individual's words and working approach can become a distinctive visual motif. Typography and arrangement can carry identity even with few images.
- Fit: an intentionally experimental personal portfolio.
- Constraint: an entry gate adds a step before useful content. Keep the offer and main action directly reachable in service and product sites. The screenshot establishes neither rotation nor an exact font identity.

### R04 — Parallel Web Systems: show a specific task

- Evidence: [Refero case](https://styles.refero.design/style/32845f27-6b24-48be-af25-8e664f826b30).
- Observed: a white canvas, a left-aligned sans-serif message, and limited orange emphasis lead into an input demonstration over a blue cloud-like field. Research, Monitor, Extract, and Search choices frame a specific competitive-research task.
- Transfer: use an understandable input and task to explain a product. Shape the visual demonstration around what the visitor might actually do.
- Fit: an AI or software product with a simple task that can be presented clearly.
- Constraint: a static screenshot does not establish a connected model, data source, or backend. Label prototype behavior accurately and write product claims from actual capabilities.

### R05 — Genie Studio: a consistent illustrated vocabulary

- Evidence: [Refero case](https://styles.refero.design/style/2ffd50d4-93b7-4acf-9bc2-e86e61b63f27).
- Observed: a pale blue canvas carries a centered headline, short explanation, and dark signup action. A palette, envelope, clouds, lamp, and small figure surround the central content while leaving its reading area clear.
- Transfer: choose related shapes, materials, and colors for original illustrations. Repeat that vocabulary with intent and preserve a clear reading and action area.
- Fit: a product or creator whose character benefits from a friendly illustrated treatment.
- Constraint: a serious service may need a different tone. Unrelated emoji or inconsistent illustrations will not reproduce the coherence of the reference.

### Supplement and interpretation checks

- [teenage engineering](https://styles.refero.design/style/aecf9dda-5cba-4dc7-9e73-59b65d895cdf): the inspected capture is dominated by a close view of product packaging, with a cookie panel obscuring part of it. It suggests a way to let a physical product carry the visual identity; it does not support conclusions about the whole navigation or purchase flow.
- Refero's generated descriptions sometimes differ from the inspected capture. The Parallel description calls its type serif, while the viewed heading is sans-serif. The Régis description supplies a font and motion interpretation that the screenshot cannot establish. Exact values, font identities, and interactions therefore remain verification tasks rather than imported facts.
- Refero's DESIGN.md entries can help formulate a direction, but their brand-specific recommendations should stay attached to that particular reference. They do not become rules for every Begin website.

## First application to Begin

These are proposed directions to test, not completed changes or accepted visual specifications. Each direction uses one principal reference and borrows only an identified decision from a supporting case.

| Target | Principal reference; supporting decision | First planned change | Content needed; workable alternative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `service` introduction and engagement process | YLLW for offer/action grouping; Fiasco only for explaining stages | Draft an opening with a specific audience, service, and contact action; follow it with the actual engagement steps. | Owned portrait or work scene and an accurate process; with no photographs, use a strong typographic introduction and an original diagram of the actual process. |
| `portfolio` selected work | Fiasco for project sequence and captions; MOUTHWASH only for featured-project scale | Give each project a brief, the creator's role, and a useful artifact; nominate one featured project. | Owned work; self-initiated work can be clearly labeled as a concept, with the actual design decisions explained. |
| `product` introduction and demonstration | Remote for explanation/demo separation; Mixpanel only for product evidence | Explain one planning task, then show how the existing live task demonstration completes it. | A working product view; an early concept can use an original labeled prototype and state which actions work locally. |
| A future purchase comparison | Choose Equals for a few clear choices or incident.io for detailed feature comparison | Determine the buyer's decision and real plan differences before drawing pricing cards or a table. | Actual offers and terms; omit a pricing comparison until there is a meaningful choice to explain. |

Refero adds alternatives to test alongside those initial routes:

- **Portfolio with rich work:** Grafik supplies a compact gallery direction; compare it with Fiasco's spaced, explanatory sequence using the same project content.
- **Service with strong photography:** Aspelin Reitan supplies an image-led opening; compare it with YLLW's direct offer/action composition using the same service message.
- **Product with a simple input task:** Parallel supplies a task-led demonstration; compare it with Remote's overview using the same working feature.
- **Product with a friendly character:** Genie supplies an illustrated direction when a coherent original asset set can support it.
- **Experimental personal site:** Régis supplies a typography-led motif; keep useful content directly accessible in the Begin interpretation.

For each new or revised starter, prepare a short design brief: audience, primary task, content/assets, selected references, decisions borrowed, original character, and review criteria. Implement original source and use assets the owner can publish. Review the result with realistic content and a second, different website brief to assess whether the approach transfers.

The second brief should be an independent tutor building a first website: accurate services, teaching approach, one labeled example lesson, and a contact method, with no client logos, awards, testimonials, or professional photographs. This checks whether the pack can guide an ordinary website owner with limited assets. Inspect reading order and contact at the narrowest supported width and breakpoint neighbors; for the portfolio inspect project order and image crops; for the product inspect demonstration scaling and usable controls. Maintain the pack's existing keyboard and touch checks.

No starter has been redesigned against this shortlist yet. The next implementation should demonstrate stronger composition and content, then verify phone/desktop behavior and the main action. A reference selection alone is not a quality result.
